MIT 2.007 Design Competition Robot

Scored the highest points in the semester in MIT's iconic robot design class, building a robot from the ground up with diverse and robust capabilities.

The final robot, enhanced with personalized design choices.
The robot's functions included automated lever flicking at a precisely timed 1 Hz, a motor-controlled hinge joint to keep the robot within the delineated size, and a high-torque gear spinner.
The lever flicker folded up to preserve space.
The electronics included an Arduino and a small breadboard containing a low-pass filter to smooth PWM signals from the remote control so the Arduino could interpret them as on/off actions, an op-amp speaker amplifier, a photoresistor for contactless activation, and Arduino controls for all servos/motors.
CAD built alongside the physical robot.

ML-based Fantasy Football Score Predictor + Mock Draft Simulator

As a fun personal project to prepare for my fantasy football league, implemented a player score predictor and a mock draft tool. Pulled NFL data via a free API, parsed databases into proper features, and iterated through a variety of ML models to predict player scores, evaluating each one on the given data. Built a front-end for selecting which stats to use as features, and a separate front-end for a fun mock draft tool where the user could draft a team alongside my model.

Physics Analysis on Potential Particle Findings at a New Proposed Collider

Conducted an analysis, using advanced statistical methods, including the ROOT library and Python and C++ repositories, to look for potential signatures of Axion-Like Particles at a proposed future collider, the FCC (Future Circular Collider). Axion-Like Particles are fundamental to answering some of the questions left unanswered by the Standard Model, including the nature of dark matter, and this analysis was part of ongoing projects to explore the potential of future colliders to answer these questions.
